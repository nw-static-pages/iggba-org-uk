RTS 14: the design rules that changed online slots in Great Britain

Since 31 October 2021 every online slot offered under a Gambling Commission licence has had to follow a set of product-design rules known as RTS 14. They do not touch RTP or volatility. They govern pace, distraction and honesty of feedback — the things that make a slot feel faster or luckier than it is.

Last reviewed: 21 September 2026 · Author: Eleanor Whitlock

The rules, clause by clause

Clause Requirement (paraphrased) What it means at the reels
RTS 14C No functionality that facilitates playing multiple games at the same time No split-screen or multi-window slots; one game per session
RTS 14D A minimum of 2.5 seconds from the start of a game until the next game cycle can begin The spin cannot be shorter than 2.5 s however hard you tap
RTS 14E Features such as turbo, quick spin and slam stop are not permitted No "skip animation" button; reels cannot be stopped early
RTS 14F The system must not celebrate a return that is less than or equal to the total stake A 20p return on a £1 spin is shown as a number, not fanfared as a win
RTS 14G A minimum of 5 seconds per game cycle for other casino games (excluding slots and peer-to-peer poker) Roulette and blackjack rounds have their own floor

Alongside RTS 14 the same 2021 package removed autoplay from online slots, required the net position (money won or lost in the session) and elapsed time to be displayed, and banned reverse withdrawals — cancelling a withdrawal to keep playing.

Why 2.5 seconds

Speed of play is one of the best-evidenced drivers of harm in machine gambling: the more outcomes per minute, the more money moves and the less time there is to notice it. A 2.5-second floor limits a slot to 24 spins a minute at most; turbo modes on pre-2021 games could run two to three times faster. The rule does not slow a careful player at all — it removes the option of playing faster than you can think.

Why "losses disguised as wins" matter

On low-volatility games a large share of "wins" return less than the stake. Before RTS 14F a 20p return on a £1 spin could trigger the same lights and sound as a real win, and research on land-based machines found players rated sessions with many such events as more successful than the arithmetic justified. The clause does not ban showing the return; it bans celebrating it.

What the rules do not do

  • They do not change the maths. RTP, hit frequency and volatility are the studio's choices within RTS 3's disclosure rules, not RTS 14's.
  • They do not apply to unlicensed sites. A site offering turbo spins or autoplay to GB customers is telling you something about its licence.

For studios and operators

RTS 14 is why the "UK version" of a slot is often a separate build: same art, same maths, different client. Studios that ship one global client typically detect the licence jurisdiction and switch the pace and feedback settings; operators are responsible for deploying the compliant build and the Commission tests for it.

Quick answers

Can I still see the reels stop quickly? The animation can be short; the cycle cannot be under 2.5 seconds.

Why does my slot show a running total? That is the net-position display from the same 2021 package. It is meant to be visible, not tucked in a menu.

Do these rules apply to live casino? RTS 14G's 5-second rule applies to casino games generally; live dealer rounds already run far slower than that.

Sources: Gambling Commission, RTS 14: responsible product design (published 2 February 2021, last updated 12 January 2026); Gambling Commission, package of changes which make online games safer by design (2 February 2021, in force 31 October 2021).
